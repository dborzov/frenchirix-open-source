## Rules every puzzle type follows

From intent.md, "Puzzles", as the code must honour them:

- **It cannot be failed.** A wrong move shakes, bounces, plays a sound and lets the kid try again. After `WRONG_BEFORE_REVEAL` (`shared/rules.ts`) wrong moves on the same item the answer is given away visibly.
- **It is about the French audio and its meaning.** Every round makes the kid hear French from the scene and connect it to its meaning. Anything French that is tapped loops its audio and stops whatever looped before.
- **The answers stay on the page**: every unit the puzzle uses is in a line of its `helpLineIds` (the validator checks it), and opening the help strip is never punished.
- **It is a real mini-game**: rich animation, and a reward moment at the end (an effect and a sound).
- **It works by touch** on a phone in portrait and an iPad in either orientation: both layouts, tap targets of at least 44 px, nothing on hover only.
- It must not assume it is the first puzzle the kid sees: a continued practice starts in the middle.
- **No two choices of one round are close in meaning** (below, "Choices"): a kid often hears only a fragment of French out of its line, so offering « Un monde » both "a world" and "in this world" is a trap, not a puzzle.
- **Every wait has an end.** Whatever the view waits for before it moves on (a play's `onEnd`, an answer from the server, the recorder) can fail or never come: a load stalls (it becomes an error only after `MEDIA_TIMEOUT_MS`, 25 s), another sound takes the one voice, the server answers 503 once and then works, the page is hidden mid-recording. Continue once on the end, the error or a timer, whichever comes first (for a play: `onceDone` from `web/src/audio`, below), and keep one enabled control that moves on at every step besides « Je ne peux pas parler ». `npm run check:liveness` plays every view through such outcomes ([checks.md](checks.md), "check:liveness"); give the view's root a `data-step` or `data-phase` so its findings say where it stopped.

## The logic

- **`build(ctx)`** returns a puzzle or `null` when the scene cannot support it (the composer then picks another type). Use `ctx.rng` for every random choice. Prefer lines not in `ctx.usedLineIds` (sort them last, do not just penalise them) and add the line you used. Honour `ctx.targetLineId` (and `targetUnitIds` for unit types): build on that line or return `null`. The composer rewrites puzzle ids to `<scene>:p<k>:<type>`; do not rely on your own.
- **`questionId`**: a type that shows a scene question picks it among `ctx.allowedQuestionIds` (undefined = all allowed; an empty set = none, return `null`) and sets `questionId`.
- **`helpLineIds`** holds every line whose French the puzzle uses; **`unitIds`** the units it exercises (key units, `unit.key`, when there are any).
- **`reduce`** is pure: never mutate the state you get (the bots check). A wrong action counts a mistake and never ends the puzzle. After `WRONG_BEFORE_REVEAL` wrong moves on one item the state must let the view give the answer away (listen-pick makes the right choice glow).
- **`missedUnitIds`**: the units the kid got wrong (weak spots). **`solve`**: the next move of a perfect player.
- **`wrongAction(puzzle, state, rng)`** (`BotHooks` in `shared/practice/bot.ts`): a wrong move a kid could make now, or `null` when none is left. Every type has one except `watch`; without it the clumsy bot plays perfectly and `check:practices` prints a warning. Declare the logic as `PuzzleType<T, S, A> & BotHooks<T, S, A>`.
- **`validate(puzzle, scene)`**: every problem a broken puzzle could have, as strings, including what only this type knows (a correct answer is among the choices). Wrong choices follow "Choices" below, and `validate` checks it, so `check:practices` enforces it on every release scene at every level.
- **`meta`**: `role` and `scope` decide the angle the composer uses it for (`angleOf`); `minLevel`/`maxLevel` the levels it suits; `needsMic` for speaking types (left out with speaking off); `weight` how often it is picked; `name`, `purpose` and `depth` are required, unique and shown on `/dev`.
- **Lines with `approxTimes`** have estimated unit and word windows. A type whose point is exact word timing (karaoke-style word puzzles, single-word loops) skips them (`hasExactTimes(line)` in `web/src/scene/timing.ts`, or `line.approxTimes` in shared code).
- Content text (`line.en`, `unit.en`) is data, never a string for `t()`. Show a line's English with `displayEn()` from `listen-pick.ts`: it joins dialogue turns and lyric breaks into one readable line.

Check: `npx vitest run shared/puzzles/<type>.test.ts`, then `npm run check:practices`.

## Choices

The user, after a catapult on "Rien de mieux" offered « Un monde » the signs "In this world" and "a world": options "too similar and natural to confuse, especially taking into account that you only hear it as a small fragment of audio outside of the full context". So every puzzle type that offers choices builds them, and validates them, by one rule: **no two choices of one round are close in meaning**: not a wrong one to the right one, and not two wrong ones to each other (« romain » offered both "a world" and "in this world" confuses too). Helpers in `shared/puzzles/distinct.ts` (content words, no imports) and `arcade.ts`:

- **Short choices** (a unit's English: `catapult`, `vrai-faux`, `memory`, `match`; a unit's French: `fill-blanks`, whose distractors share no French content word with a blank or with each other, and have no English close to a blank's or to each other's). No two share a content word (`closeInMeaning`: `nearlyEqual`, or `sharedEnWords`). Content words leave out articles, prepositions, pronouns, auxiliaries, conjunctions and interjections (the lists in `distinct.ts`), and count simple plural and inflected forms as the same word ("worlds", "running"/"runs"). Words of one **synonym family** count as one word too (`SYNONYMS` in `distinct.ts`, a short list built from what the release's unit glosses contain: good, better, best, great, wonderful, nice, fine…; okay, all right, very well…; small, little, tiny; and a dozen more), so "how good it is" is never offered with "wonderful" or "there is nothing better", nor "ah okay" with "very well"; "well" alone is an interjection and counts for nothing. Whole lines do not use the families (their threshold counts words). Over the release the families removed 373 of about 45,000 rounds and boards with two choices of one family; the build null rates stayed the same (vrai-faux 33.4 % → 33.5 %, memory 30.9 % → 31.0 %). A wrong one is never the English of the same French or of French containing it: a candidate whose French shares a content word with the answer's (or with another wrong one's) is skipped (`frenchRelated`, accents folded), and so is one close to the English of any unit of the scene with related French (`relatedGlosses`). `pickDistractors(answer, unitGlossPools(ctx, rng), n, relatedGlosses(scene, unit))` does all of it; `checkChoices(type, where, choices, answer, related)` validates it.
- **Pairs where every side is a choice for every other** (`memory`, `match`): no two pairs close in meaning or with related French (`pickDistinctUnits(…, { apart: true })`; `clash` in `match.ts`).
- **Whole lines** (`listen-pick`, `line-meaning`). Lines are heard in full and naturally share a word or two, so the threshold is looser: two lines (the right one and a wrong one, or two wrong ones) are too close when they share **more than half** of the shorter line's content words (`linesShareMeaning`, `LINE_SHARED_MAX`): "We feel good, it is hot" and "We feel good in the land of Panono" (2 of 4) may stand side by side; "Ah yes, it's good" and "Yeah, yeah, it's good" may not. `linesCloseInMeaning` adds `linesConfusable`. `listen-pick` also skips the English of any line whose French says mostly the same words as the line's (`frLinesShareWords`: a repeated verse translated another way would be a second right answer).
- **A new puzzle type with choices** uses these helpers in `build` and checks them in `validate`. When the rule leaves a type unable to build on a scene, `build` returns `null` and the composer covers the scene with other types; `check:practices` prints each type's null rate and the lines left under `MIN_ANGLES_PER_LINE` angles; compare them before and after a change to the rule.

## The view

The view is a `React.FC<PuzzleViewProps<T, S, A>>` with `{ puzzle, state, dispatch, scene, practiceCtx }`.

- `puzzle` and `state` come from the logic; the shell holds the state and runs `dispatch(action)` through `reduce`. Keep only presentation in React state, never the puzzle's truth.
- `practiceCtx` (`PracticeCtx` in `web/src/puzzles/registry.ts`): `layout` (`phone` or `tablet`), `speak`, `puzzleIndex`/`puzzleCount`, `openHelp()` (opens the help strip, counts a hint), `hint()` (counts one hint; pass it to a strip the puzzle draws itself), `next()` (move on without the shell's continue bar), `setSpeak(false)` ("Je ne peux pas parler"), `dev` (true on the `/dev` page that runs one puzzle).
- **When `isDone` turns true the shell takes over**: it slides up the continue bar with a cheer scaled to the mistakes (`PARFAIT !` when perfect), plays a whoosh, flashes a combo after two perfect puzzles in a row, and records the outcome. The view plays only its own reward on the winning move (`fx.right(el)`). Do not draw your own continue button; call `next()` only if the puzzle should move on by itself.
- **Help mode**, given at registration: `'open'` (default) when the puzzle already shows the French, `'closed'` when the strip would give the round away (behind a help button; opening it counts a hint), `'none'` when the puzzle is itself the whole scene. Today every type is `'closed'` except `watch` and `place-words` (`'none'`). On a phone the help strip is a bottom drawer; on a tablet it sits in the scene pane beside the puzzle (under the slideshow in landscape, beside it above the puzzle when the tablet is upright; "Layouts" below).
- On a tablet the scene pane already shows the slideshow following the audio; a puzzle shows its own picture only on a phone (listen-pick: `{!tablet && <Slideshow … />}`).
- The puzzle column scrolls; keep the main choices visible on a 390×844 phone without scrolling when you can. On an upright tablet the pane is the whole width (about 740–790 px), on a landscape one about 500 px: a view's `tablet` styles serve both.

## The practice screen (the shell)

`web/src/practice/`: `PracticePlayer` takes the `PracticePlan` from `POST /api/practices/start`, composes the practice from its seed (`composePractice`) and starts at `resumeIndex(done, length, same)`; `PracticeRunner` plays it from there to the last puzzle in one run: no sittings, no breaks.

- **Where am I** (intent.md, "Practices and levels"). The header's row: close, what the practice is (`LevelTag`: the medal and name of the level it is for, in the level's colour, or « Rejouer » / "Replay" for a replay; under it the scene's French title; on a tablet the scene's picture before them), the microphone switch (while speaking rounds lie ahead, and whenever speaking is off), mute, and the « Quelque chose ne va pas ? » flag. Under it the track: a progress bar that fills as puzzles are done (a tick every five puzzles, the slot of the puzzle on screen blinking), « Défi n sur N » / "Puzzle n of N" on it, and a tag with how many are left (« Encore 21 », « Le dernier ! », « Fini ! »). A finished puzzle counts at once, before « Continuer ». The bar's `aria-valuenow` is the number of finished puzzles (the e2e specs read it as the saved position). The puzzle's name is a caption on the puzzle pane's top edge. The runner takes `level` and `replay` from the plan (`/dev/practice?…&replay=1` shows a replay); without them, the practice's entry.
- **Which lines a puzzle is about** is not in the header: the composer mixes lines on purpose (variety), so a line mark would jump at every puzzle and say nothing about how far the kid is; the help strip and the slideshow, which rests on the puzzle's first line, already show it.
- **Save, then continue.** When a puzzle is done (`isDone`), the shell records its outcome and sends `{ done, total, outcomes }` with `POST /api/practices/:id/progress` once, while the continue bar slams in; after the last puzzle it sends the completion (`POST /api/practices/:id/complete`) instead. The kid moves on only once the save is answered: « Continuer » tapped earlier waits for it (the button shows it is busy). A failed save (offline, a timeout, a server error) replaces the continue bar with the error panel (`ErrorPanel variant="bar"`: the message, « Réessayer », the admin's detail line); « Réessayer » sends the same save again and moves on once it is saved. Nothing is queued or retried behind the kid's back. The results show only once the completion is saved. An answer `practice-closed` (finished on another device or tab, or the answer to an earlier try got lost) refreshes the state and shows the results as « Déjà enregistré ! », never « Oups ». `practice/storage.ts` keeps only this tab's position (sessionStorage) for a reload.
- **Leaving and continuing.** Close always asks « Une pause ? » (everything finished is saved) with « Je joue encore ! » and « Je continue plus tard »; leaving keeps the count of finished puzzles, never the state inside a puzzle, and the practice continues at the next unfinished puzzle from Home, the scene page or another device.
- **Loading.** From the plan to the first puzzle the screen is the media gate ("Loading media" below): the scene's data, then its audio and first frames, with a progress bar; once the first puzzle shows, the practice's clips are fetched ahead.
- **Opening.** The screen that starts a practice hands the fresh plan over once (`{ plan, fresh: true }` in the navigation state). Any other way in (the back button, a reload, history) asks the server first with `{ kind: 'resume' }`: a finished practice says so and goes Home; an open one continues at the server's `done`. A practice opened with every puzzle done (the completion's answer was lost earlier) sends the completion at once.
- **The results** (`Results.tsx`): the level tag on the cover, the stars, the XP of the whole practice (`rewards.xp` plus `creditedXp`, what its progress saves credited), the streak (with « Série complète ! » when this practice made today a full streak day, `streak.full`), « Quête réussie ! » when it did one of today's quests (`quest`), the scene's level going up (or when the next level is due, after a replay), achievements; the button opens the quest's chest when there is one (`chest`), else goes Home. The results scroll; the button sticks to the bottom of the screen, so on a phone it is in reach before the panels under the fold are scrolled to.
- **Layouts.** The body never scrolls itself: the puzzle column scrolls (and the scene pane on a tablet), keyed by the puzzle so each puzzle starts at the top, and the puzzle pane fills the column's height, so a short puzzle is a whole panel, not a box over empty page. Phone: the puzzle column, the help strip as a bottom drawer; the puzzle stays at the top of its pane (the drawer changes the pane's height). Tablet in landscape: the scene pane (slideshow, help strip) on the left, the puzzle on the right. Tablet held upright (taller than wide, `@media (orientation: portrait)`): the slideshow (larger) and the help strip side by side on top, the help strip at the slideshow's height (scrolling inside when open), and the puzzle under them across the whole width. On a tablet a short puzzle sits in the middle of its pane (auto margins; a tall one starts at the top and scrolls). The header stays two short rows on a 375 px phone.
- **A crash in a puzzle view** is caught around the puzzle (`ErrorBoundary`): the header stays, the puzzle pane shows « Ce défi s'est bloqué », the detail line, « Réessayer » (remounts the puzzle from its start) and Home. It is logged with `console.error('[frx] crash …')` and as a `crash` event in the activity log. This is no skip button: stuck puzzles are caught during development ([checks.md](checks.md)).
- **Hints** are counted (into `PuzzleOutcome.hints`, which marks the puzzle's units as weak) only where the French starts hidden: opening a help strip that starts closed, or tapping a box or a line loop inside it; and whenever the puzzle calls `hint()` or `openHelp()`. Taps in a strip that starts open count nothing unless the puzzle passes `hint`. Hints only change the stars and the weak-spot statistics.
- **The log.** The first practice shows the flag's tip under the header, in the flow ([app-architecture.md](app-architecture.md), "Activity log and reports"). The shell logs `practice-open`, `puzzle-show`, `puzzle-done` and `practice-results`, and tells the log which puzzle is on screen (`setPuzzleWhere`).
- The shell stops the French when a puzzle ends, before the next view mounts.

## Audio

The engine is `web/src/audio/` (Web Audio, one French voice at a time, a separate bus for effects):

```ts
import { audio, useIsPlaying, useStopAudioOnUnmount } from '../../audio';
import { lineWindow, loopWindowFor, tags } from '../../scene/timing';
const u = loopWindowFor(line, unit);   // a unit: the whole line when the line has approxTimes
audio.playRange(scene.audio.src, u.startMs, u.endMs, { loop: true, tag: tags.unit(unit.id) });
audio.play(unit.drill!);               // a word drill or generated speech: the whole file, once
useStopAudioOnUnmount();               // every puzzle view calls this
```

- Any play stops the previous one, with a short fade. A loop repeats with a 350 ms gap (`gapMs`), like a chant. `PlayOptions` in `audio/engine.ts` lists `rate`, `onEnd`, `onIteration` and the rest; hooks such as `useIsPlaying(tag)` and `useAudioClock(select)` (pass a selector returning a primitive) let views follow playback.
- **A play a step waits on** (the model before the microphone, the lines before the kid's turn, a listen-back): `audio.playRange(src, a, b, { tag, ...onceDone(next, b - a, later) })` calls `next` once, on the play's end, its error, or a timer at its length plus `PLAY_SLACK_MS` (2.5 s), and clears the timer once it went on. `later(fn, ms)` is the view's own timer returning its handle (`useLater` in `web/src/puzzles/useLater.ts`), so nothing starts on a puzzle that is gone. Never wait on `onEnd` alone: a stopped or replaced play (a tapped box takes the voice) never reports its end, and a stalled load reports nothing.
- **Loop style D is the default for every loop** (the user chose it by ear, intent.md). The window is padded 120 ms before and 200 ms after (clamped to the clip), raised-cosine fades fill the padding, and both cut points move to the quietest moment inside it (an RMS scan, cached per window for the session; the first repetition uses the padded window until the scan is ready). The unit itself plays at full volume. Non-looping plays use short linear fades. `audio/loopStyle.ts` keeps styles A to E only for comparison on `/dev/loops`; pass `loopStyle` only there.
- Always use `loopWindowFor(line, unit)` for a unit's loop, never `unit.startMs/endMs` directly.
- Start a loop in a `useEffect` keyed on the puzzle id; the kid's first tap has already unlocked audio. Times are milliseconds from the start of the scene's audio file.
- **Mute** (`audio.setMuted`, `useMuted()`, the speaker button in the shell and the practice header) silences everything through the master gain and is remembered on the device. While muted, plays still run silently (timing, `onEnd` and microphone rounds keep working), effects are skipped, and nothing may start by itself: guard every autostart with `if (!audio.isMuted())`. A puzzle never shows an error because sound is off.
- The engine sets the audio session to playback (when the browser has `navigator.audioSession`) and to play-and-record only while the microphone is open. iOS behaviour is unverified ([app-status.md](app-status.md)).
- **An overlay**: `PlayOptions.overlay` (`{ src, offsetMs, gain? }`) plays a second clip in time with the play, its first sample at `offsetMs` of the clip: each iteration schedules the part of the overlay under it (`overlaySpan` in `shared/karaoke.ts`) through the iteration's own envelope, so seeks, loops, fades and stops carry it along. The overlay never holds the play back: the clip starts as soon as it is ready, and the overlay joins the iterations under way when it has loaded (at once when it is in the cache); one that cannot be loaded calls its `onError` and the play goes on without it. `getState().overlay` names it only once it is in the mix. The karaoke plays a kid's recording over its song this way. `clipHeardAt(clipMs)` tells when a position of the current play is heard, in `performance.now()` ms (the output latency the browser reports included).
- Loops heard are counted by the engine and sent to `POST /api/events` in batches; a puzzle does nothing for it. Count other events with `countEvent(stat, ref)` from `audio/events.ts`.

## Loading media

What the user decided (intent.md, "When something goes wrong", 2026-11-17): on a slow or spotty connection a screen must say that it is loading, with a progress bar, rather than look broken. Code: `web/src/media/` (`fetchMedia.ts`, `loadMedia.ts`, `MediaGate.tsx`) and the engine's `load`.

- **Every media file is fetched by `fetchMedia`** (the engine's clips, the gate's frames, the fetch ahead): progress in bytes as they arrive (`Bytes`: `loaded`, and `total` from `Content-Length`, null when the answer has none or is compressed), and an abort after `MEDIA_TIMEOUT_MS` (25 s) **without a byte**, so a stall becomes an error while a slow load that keeps receiving is never cut. A failure is a `MediaLoadError` (`src`, `status`, `reason`: `timeout`, `offline`, `http`, `decode`); the engine's `AudioLoadError` is one. A failed load leaves nothing in the engine's cache, so the next try fetches again.
- **The engine API**: `audio.load(src, onProgress?)` fetches and decodes one clip into the cache (shared with a load already under way; instant from the cache) and rejects with an `AudioLoadError`; `audio.isLoaded(src)`; `preload` as before. `getState().loading` is true while the current play's clip is on its way (`playing` is already true then).
- **The media gate** (`MediaGate`, one component): the practice screen (`PracticePlayer`, around the whole runner, from the scene's data on), the scene page (around the player, keyed by the scene) and the karaoke screen (the full player only; Home's card is not gated) show their content only once **what the first moment needs** is in: the scene's or song's audio and its first `FIRST_FRAMES` (3) frames (`firstMoment`). Nothing else: not the practice's clips, not the kid's recording (the song never waits for it). Until then: nothing for `GATE_DELAY_MS` (300 ms, so a cached load passes without a flash; from the cache the gate renders its content at once), then a cream panel with « Chargement… » and a progress bar; after `MEDIA_SLOW_MS` (8 s) the line « Connexion lente… on charge encore. » and a Home button (the practice screen has no other way out). A failed file (a stall included) shows the error panel in place (`ErrorPanel`: the message for the kind, the detail line, « Réessayer », Home); « Réessayer » is the only retry. While the gate is up, the scene page's action is not a sticky bar but sits in the page under it (`.gated`), so it never covers the error panel's detail line and copy button. The karaoke's autoplay waits for the gate (`onReady`).
- **Progress is honest**: by bytes when every file of the gate said its size (`data-progress-by="bytes"`; the local Worker's media answers carry `Content-Length`; production's were not checked behind the gate cookie, and without it the bar counts files), else by files done out of files needed; the bar never runs back. No invented animation (the stripes on the bar's fill only decorate a real width).
- **Errors**: `kindOf` maps a `MediaLoadError` to `timeout`, `offline` or `server`; `errorMessage` uses `common.fail.timeout` / `offline`, or `common.fail.media` for an HTTP or decoding failure; `errorDetail` gives `FRX-ERR media <reason> <status> GET <src> msg="…" build=… at=…`. Logged as `media-fail` (`where`: `gate` or `play`).
- **An honest play button**: a control whose play is still loading shows the hopping dots (`IconButton busy`, and the same in `LoopButton`, `place-words`' play button and `say-choice`'s speaker; `useIsLoading(tag)` in `audio/`), never "pause" or the speaker that means "playing", with the label « Le son arrive… »; a tap on it does not cancel. A play whose clip fails while it is the current one calls `Engine.onPlayError`, which `main.tsx` turns into a toast « Ce son n'arrive pas… » with the detail line (once per clip in 5 s); tapping again is the retry. Exception, by the user's choice: the `question` puzzle's question and answer clips pass `quiet: true` and keep their silent fallback to the line's audio.
- **Fetch ahead, this practice only**: once the first puzzle is on screen, `PracticePlayer` fetches the rest of the practice's clips (`practiceClips`: question and answer clips by the puzzle's `questionId`, and every `/media/` path a puzzle carries: alternates, drills, a microphone puzzle's replacement) in puzzle order from the puzzle it starts at, three at a time (`fetchAhead`). Only the download: the bytes land in the browser's HTTP cache (media URLs are cached hard) and the engine decodes a clip when it plays it, so nothing counts against the engine's memory cap. A failure stays quiet; the play loads the clip again and says so if it fails. Nothing is fetched for other scenes.
- Tests: `web/src/media/media.test.ts` (progress, the stall timeout, retry after a failure, the play-error hook, the gate's progress, the detail line, `practiceClips`), `web/src/scene/busy.test.tsx` (the scene player's busy button); `e2e/media.worker.spec.ts` (throttled and blocked media; every part of the error panel on the practice screen, the scene page and the karaoke screen can be scrolled to and is not covered; screenshots in `E2E_SHOTS`, default `test-results/media`).

## The help strip and `DialogueStrip`

The promise "the answers are always on the page" is kept by the shell: every puzzle screen has a help strip, the lines of `helpLineIds` drawn as a `DialogueStrip` (`web/src/scene/DialogueStrip`). Each unit is a coloured box; a tap pops it, loops its audio (via `loopWindowFor`) and opens the hint bubble with its English and note; tapping it again stops. A loop button per line loops the line. A scene note carries one asterisk, on the last unit of its phrase (a phrase over three boxes once drew three asterisks that opened the same text), which opens the note as a caption; a hidden unit (a blank still to fill) shows none until its word is in place, since the note would name the hidden word. While the scene's audio plays, the spoken word lights up (a whole line when it has `approxTimes`).

`<DialogueStrip scene={scene} lineIds={[line.id]} onHint={practiceCtx.hint} />`. Props puzzles use: `masked`, `maskStyle` (`'initial'`: first letters plus dots; `'blank'`), `maskFill` (hide units and show what the kid put in a blank); `onUnitTap(unitId)` with `loopOnTap={false}` or `bubbles={false}` when a tap means something else; `activeUnitId`, `unitClassName(unit, line)` for selection and right/wrong colouring; `onHint(kind, id)` on every revealing tap; `disabled`, `compact`, `showEnglish`, `linePlay`, `followAudio`.

**A line to say, word by word**: draw it as `<DialogueStrip … wordMarks={marks} />`, never as plain text, so its boxes stay tappable. `wordMarks` maps `'<unit id>#<word index>'` to `'matched' | 'missed' | 'current'`; `lineWordKeys(line)` gives the keys in spoken order, to pair with the server's `matched` list. Matched words turn green, missed ones get a wavy red underline, the word being listened for a dashed outline.

**A whole scene: line panels** (`linePanels`; the scene player turns it on, `place-words` draws the same panels itself). Each line is a panel in its line's colour, the colour of its segment in the playback bar: the n-th line takes `--unit-(n mod 6 + 1)` (`lineColorVars` in `web/src/scene/lineColors.ts`; the look in `LinePanel.module.css`). By default the panel is a tint (the colour under a white veil, so `background-color` stays the line's colour for the contrast check), the line being said is the full colour, lifted. The boxes of a panel cycle through the five other colours (`unitOnLineColorVars`), never the panel's, and the speaker tag is ink, so nothing melts into its panel. A tap on the panel outside a box (a transparent button behind the content, `data-line-loop`) loops the line, stopping what looped before: the panel slams in, wears a dashed frame and a loop tag (`data-looping`), and the bar marks the line. Tapping it again lets the scene play on from that line (`onLineSeek`; without it, stop). Another line, the play button or the bar end it too; a tapped box loops itself as always. A strip of a few lines (the help strip) keeps the plain rows: there is no bar to match.

`ScenePlayer` (slideshow, strip, big play button, looping the scene) and `Slideshow` (`timeMs` fixed, or `audioSrc` to follow the audio) are in the same folder. The scene player's big button pauses whatever of the scene plays (the scene, a looping line or box) and plays the scene on from the line last looped.

## The hint bubble

`web/src/scene/hint.ts` is the one meaning balloon for every piece of French tapped for its meaning, so every puzzle behaves the same (intent.md, "Puzzles"):

1. It shows only the English in that sense (and the unit's note), never the French.
2. Its tail touches the tapped box: just below it, or above when there is no room, never over it, never off screen, following the box when the layout moves; it ignores a looping box's pulse.
3. Tapping the bubble hides it and the tap goes no further (a tap that went through once pressed "Je ne peux pas parler" under a bubble).
4. Any tap, drag, wheel or key elsewhere hides it, and that input still does what it does. One bubble at a time.

Several notes in one bubble each take their own block, with a dashed rule between them. The bubble is never taller than the room on the roomier side of its box (`hint.ts` sets its `max-height` as it places it): a long note scrolls inside the bubble, and scrolling it does not close it (a 375×667 phone once cut the top off the longest note, 469 characters, with no way to read it).

Use `hint.toggle(anchorElement, { en, note })` on the tapped box, `useHintAnchor()` to draw the anchored box as selected, `hint.hide()` when a round changes. `DialogueStrip`, `GlossText`, karaoke and `place-words` already use it. Never draw a meaning balloon of your own; a puzzle's content balloons (a question, the English of a round) are not hints. For tests: one `[data-bubble]` inside `[data-hint-layer]`, whose `data-tip-x`/`data-tip-y` give the tail tip.

## The playback bar

`web/src/scene/SceneBar`, wherever a whole scene or song plays: a bar the length of the audio, one coloured segment per line (a thick mark where the speaker changes), a moving playhead, tap or drag to seek, the current line named. `<SceneBar lines={scene.lines} durationMs={scene.audio.durationMs} src={scene.audio.src} onSeek={…} />`. The segments take the line colours of the line panels (`lineColorVars`), so the kid matches a segment to its line. A line looping after a tap in the dialogue is marked: its segment stands out of the track, ringed and beating (`data-bar-loop`), and the line's name carries a loop icon. By default the bar reads the looping line from the engine (a looping play tagged `line:<id>` of this clip); `loopLineId` overrides it (`place-words` passes its selected line, which stays selected while a tapped box speaks). `holdWhile(tag)` keeps the bar as it was while a play with such a tag runs; `Slideshow` takes the same prop and keeps its frame. `ScenePlayer` has it and passes `onLineSeek` to its strip, so a line's play control jumps the scene there; in the practice's help strip the line control still loops the line.

## Our own French: `GlossText` and the funny voices

Questions, answers and alternates (French that is not a scene line) are drawn with `GlossText` (`web/src/scene/GlossText`): each `GlossUnit` is a box, text between units is plain, a tapped box opens the hint bubble with its English. No audio window: these sentences are not in a scene's audio. Props: `size` (`sm`, `md`, `lg`), `tone` (`colour`, `paper` for white boxes on a coloured card, `ink`), `disabled`, `balloons`, `onUnitTap(index, unit)`, `onGloss(index, unit)` (call `practiceCtx.hint()` there if the puzzle counts it), `marks` by unit index. The boxes are `<span role="button">` whose clicks stop propagation, so a `GlossText` may sit inside a tappable card. `closeGlosses()` hides the bubble when a round changes; `wholeSentenceUnits(fr, en)` makes the one-unit fallback by hand.

Question, answer and alternate clips are plain-voice static files (`audio` in the bundle, [app-tts.md](app-tts.md)); the player makes a question funny with its `voice` preset: `audio.play(q.audio, { voice: q.voice, loop: true, onError })`. `VOICES` in `audio/engine.ts`: `squeaky` (faster, higher), `deep` (slower, lower), `wobble` (vibrato), `echo` (a short feedback delay); tune them there. A clip that is missing or cannot be decoded calls `onError` with an `AudioLoadError` and rejects `handle.ready`, nothing unhandled; then show the question silently and loop the related line of the scene (`question.lineId`). These plays pass `quiet: true`, so no "this sound won't come" toast (the user's choice; "Loading media").

## Speaking puzzles

The microphone types are `say-line`, `say-hidden`, `act-line`, `say-chain` and `say-choice`. Speaking must never frustrate or block a kid (intent.md, "Speaking"). How the server recognises and judges: [app-server.md](app-server.md).

**The microphone control** (`MicControl`, `web/src/speech/MicControl.tsx`) is the one control every speaking puzzle type shows, so a kid always knows whether they are being listened to (the user: "always clear when the microphone is listening … you can always resume and continue"). Its states (`data-mic`):

| State | Looks like | Tap |
|---|---|---|
| `idle` | a red square with the microphone, « Touche et parle ! » / « Encore une fois ! » (the try counter stamped on it) | starts a take |
| `opening` | a spinner, « J'allume le micro… », Pause | Pause cancels it |
| `listening` | the button turns yellow with a live meter inside it (red bars, green while a voice is picked up) and a red halo that grows with the voice; a blinking « Je t'écoute ! » tag; a countdown bar with the seconds left (red for the last three); « Touche quand tu as fini »; Pause | the button: done, the take is checked; Pause: the take is dropped, nothing sent |
| `thinking` | a cream square with a spinner, « Je vérifie… » (the mic is off) | nothing |
| `waiting` | the speaker, « Écoute… » (say-chain's model plays; the mic is off) | Pause (say-chain) |
| `paused` | a green square with the microphone, « Continuer » | goes on |

The meter reads the recorder's own samples (`useRecorder().onLevel`, written into a CSS variable, no re-render) and its scale is in decibels (`meterLevel`: −50 dB empty, −10 dB full), so a quiet voice still moves it. When the microphone opens or starts listening the control scrolls itself into view, and the tap-to-speak types reveal it on mount. `data-record` on the button keeps the recorder's status for the tests.

**The dock** (`web/src/speech/dock.ts`, `MicDock`): on a phone, and on a tablet held sideways (under 900 px high), every speaking type puts « Je ne peux pas parler », the note after a take and the *compact* control (the button, then its tag, Pause as an icon and the countdown on its right) in a cyan box stuck to the bottom of the puzzle, as say-choice always did; an upright tablet keeps the full control (a 9-box line and the control fit at 768×1024). So the control never scrolls away, and the French being said stays readable above it: the view marks it `data-speak-keep` (a `TargetStrip` counts), and `keepAboveDock` scrolls just enough to show it above the dock, never past its first row, when the microphone opens, on mount and whenever the dock changes. In the dock the verdict after a miss is short (what was heard, one line; the boxes not lit show what is missing) and steps aside while the microphone is open; on a phone under 760 px high the target's boxes are smaller. Measured: a 9-box say-line on 375×667 and a 9-box act-line on 390×844 and 375×667 stay whole, listening and after a miss (once, the full control under them pushed most of the line off the top). The microphone's notices and listen-back stay in the flow of the puzzle.

**Limits.** The microphone listens at most `LISTEN_MAX_MS` (10 s, `shared/puzzles/speak-core.ts`) per take, whatever the puzzle asks (`recordLimitMs` caps every type's `maxRecordMs`, the validators check it, and the recorder clamps it again). A take ends: when the kid taps the button; after `SILENCE_MS` (1.5 s, `recorder.ts`; act-line 1.8 s, say-chain 0.7 s) of silence once a voice was heard; or at the limit, and is then sent as it is (the words heard stay lit for the next try). The release's longest line lasts 5.7 s in the scene; a slow reader may not finish the few lines of 20 words or more in 10 s (5 lines), and finishes them on the next try. The microphone is open only in `opening` and `listening`, always with that control on screen; the tracks are stopped after every take, on Pause, when the puzzle ends or unmounts, and when the page is hidden: the take is dropped like Pause (no try spent; the panel says « En pause », say-chain pauses). An audio context interrupted while the page is in view (a call) still ends the take as an automatic stop, so a microphone cut every time reaches the `deaf` notice and its way out. **Nothing plays while the microphone is open** (`useRecorder`): the recorder silences the French when it opens, and any sound that starts during `opening` or `listening` is stopped at once, since it would go into the take (once, the whole line looped over the kid's voice: they had tapped the microphone while a miss's missing piece played, and the line that follows it came a few seconds later). A view's own play buttons (say-line's speaker, say-hidden's « Écouter », act-line's « Réécouter la scène ») drop the take first (`SpeakPanel`'s `dropTake`: the microphone off, nothing sent); a box tapped in the help strip during a take stays silent. **The control pauses by itself** (`MicControl`): while Pause shows, a dialog over the puzzle (« Une pause ? », the report form), the help strip opening, or the page hidden calls the view's own Pause, so the microphone never listens, nor say-chain goes on, behind them; act-line's microphone opening by itself under a dialog pauses at once. A help strip already open when a take starts does not pause it.

**A take with no voice** (the voice gate below says silent or too short) is never sent and spends no try: « Je n'ai rien entendu ! » and the control again. After two in a row the note says « Toujours rien… » and points at « Je ne peux pas parler », which turns yellow and wiggles. After `UNHEARD_LIMIT` (3) in a row the microphone's own notice comes (`useRecorder` status `deaf`: « Je n'entends pas ton micro », « Passer » and « Réessayer le micro »); a retry gets one more take before the notice comes back. So nothing ever says it heard the kid when it did not; after an unrecognised third try the pass says « Bravo ! On continue ! », not « je t'ai bien entendu ».

**The voice gate** (`lookAt` and `judgeRecording` in `web/src/speech/wav.ts`, constants `VOICE`): the client decides whether a take holds a voice before anything is sent, so silence, taps and hum never cost a Whisper request; the server's VAD filter and its "nothing heard" rule stay as the second line of defence ([app-server.md](app-server.md)). Tuned to send when in doubt: a quiet child's voice must never be dropped. On 20 ms frames of the 16 kHz take:

- **the room's floor**: the lowest of the take's 10th-percentile frame level, the median of its first 300 ms, and `FLOOR_CAP` (0.04, −28 dBFS), so a take that is all voice, or a loud room, cannot hide a voice;
- **a voiced frame**: louder than `ABS_MIN_RMS` (0.008, −42 dBFS) and than `FLOOR_RATIO` (2.5, +8 dB) times the floor, with a zero-crossing rate (counted with a small hysteresis) of at least `ZCR_MIN` (0.008 per sample, about a 64 Hz tone): mains hum and rumble cross zero too rarely, a deep adult voice (100 Hz) does not;
- **no voice** (`silent`): no run of voiced frames of `MIN_SYLLABLE_MS` (80 ms, one-frame gaps bridged): taps and clicks are shorter than a syllable, steady hum or a fan never rises 2.5 times above itself;
- **too short** (`short`): the take under 400 ms, or under `MIN_VOICE_MS` (120 ms) of voiced frames in all;
- **a voice**: trimmed to 300 ms before its first voiced frame and 400 ms after its last (smaller uploads), then sent.

Measured on synthetic takes (`wav.test.ts`: silence, room noise, 50 and 120 Hz hum, a loud fan, taps, a thump, speech-like syllables at several pitches and levels): a voice is found once it is about twice the room's level and above −42 dBFS. Untested on real children's voices: the checklist in [app-status.md](app-status.md). While recording, `LiveVoice` applies the same idea block by block (louder than 2.5 times the quietest block of the last 1.5 s): it colours the meter and drives the end-of-speech stop, so steady noise never keeps a take open for long and a kid who keeps talking is never cut off. `/dev/speaking` shows the gate's verdict for each take (the lab sends every take, silent ones too).

**`SpeakPanel`** (`web/src/speech`) does everything around the microphone for the single-target types (`say-line`, `say-hidden`, `act-line`, which share the pure core `shared/puzzles/speak-core.ts`: actions `verdict`, `unavailable`, `me`, `skip`): the first-time notice about the microphone (remembered in `localStorage` `frx.mic.intro`), the microphone control, the rules above, `POST /api/speech` with `attempt`, the feedback (what was heard, what is missing; the last verdict stays up during the next take so the control does not jump), Pause (« En pause. Touche le micro quand tu veux. ») and the listen-back round. Whenever it changes height it scrolls the next thing to tap into view (in the dock: the French above it). It waits for recognition at most `SPEECH_TIMEOUT_MS` (8 s, `send.ts`, the same in every speaking type), then the round becomes a listen-back. say-hidden passes `showMissing`, so what is still to say is masked like its tiles (first letters), never spelled out. Every play it waits on (the original before the kid's voice in listen-back, the kid's voice, `replayMissing`) goes on through `onceDone`; a view passes `playOriginal` (which forwards `onEnd` and `onError`) and `originalMs`.

**Rules that remove friction**: the words heard in any try stay lit (partial credit that builds); a try passes when the server says so or when every word has been heard across the tries; **the third try at the same target always passes**, with the same celebration and no consolation wording; failed tries one and two count a mistake (stars only), and `wrongAction` returns `null` once the next try would end the round. Only takes with a voice are tries. The target is drawn as tappable boxes (`TargetStrip`): a heard box lights up, a half-heard one is striped; after a miss, `replayMissing` plays the first piece not yet heard, then the whole target.

**Listen-back**: when the server answers anything but a verdict (`503 speech-unavailable`, `429`, a 403, offline), the round becomes the original, then the kid's own recording, then « c'est moi ! » to go on. No scoring, no penalty. A denied or missing microphone shows skip (done, no mistake) and "Je ne peux pas parler".

**The recorder** (`useRecorder(onTake, { playAlong? })`: `record(maxMs, { silenceMs, capMs, whole })`, `stop()` (done), `pause()` (drop the take, or the microphone being opened, without an error), `reset()` (after a notice); `status`, `clock` (the take's limit and start, for the countdown), `unheard`, `onLevel`) records through an AudioWorklet in its own AudioContext opened inside the tap, resamples to 16 kHz mono and encodes a WAV itself (never MediaRecorder), so every browser sends the same thing; the meter reads the same samples (no second AudioContext, no AnalyserNode). It stops looping French first. Every take that starts reaches `onTake`, or is dropped with the status back to idle: `pause()`, `reset()`, unmounting and the page hidden drop it (the recorder's `onCut`); an interrupted audio context ends it like an automatic stop with what was recorded so far, and an empty one comes back judged silent (once, a cut take was dropped without resetting the status and left every microphone puzzle on a dead stop button). Other pieces: `MicControl`, `MicNotice` (the first-time explanation, a denied, missing, failing or deaf microphone; `words` gives another screen its own wording), `CantSpeak`, `askSpeech`. Only the karaoke uses the options: `playAlong` lets sound play while the microphone is open (the song), `lazy` says nothing about a missing microphone before a tap and goes back to idle after its notice, `intro: false` skips the speaking puzzles' first-time explanation (the karaoke has its own), `capMs` raises the 10 s ceiling (`KARAOKE_MAX_MS`), `whole` keeps the take untrimmed, and an untrimmed take carries `capturedAt`, when its first sample reached the microphone (the worklet sends each block's context time).

**The types:**

- **`say-line`**: always the whole line, at every level (the user: "Say the line is to say the whole dialog line"), with the line's window as the model; the level only prefers shorter lines at first. Expression-sized speaking is `say-hidden`'s and `say-chain`'s.
- **`act-line`**: the line or two before play; « À TOI ! » slams in with the character's name; the microphone opens by itself and closes after a longer silence; the line's boxes stay lit across tries, so a long line can be finished piece by piece; on a pass the next line plays. Once the turn has come the speaking panel stays mounted: « Réécouter la scène » replays the lines before under it (remounting it lost the take and the count of takes with no voice); tapped while the microphone is open, it drops the take first, and the microphone opens again by itself after the lines. The button sits right above the speaking panel, so on an upright tablet it is in view whenever the microphone control is (the control scrolls itself into view while it listens; at the end of the view it was below the fold at every size); where the control is docked, the line is kept readable above the dock first, and the button may sit behind the dock until the kid scrolls down.
- **`say-chain`**: 4 to 6 key units, hands-free after one tap on the microphone (« C'est parti ! »): per item the model once (« Écoute… », the mic off), then the microphone, a stop after a short silence, then on by itself, or the model and the microphone again; the third try moves on. A take with no voice pauses the chain with the microphone off (« Je n'ai rien entendu ! », « Continuer »), so it never keeps listening to nobody. Pause is there at every running step and drops what was under way (its timers, the take, a late answer); Continue starts the item again from its model; hiding the page pauses too. Without recognition (a 503, or no answer within `SPEECH_TIMEOUT_MS`, 8 s, as the other types) that item becomes a listen-back and the next item asks again, so a server that answers again scores again (the logic takes a verdict or a timeout in either mode; `timeout` is now only the third try, which moves on without waiting). Intent.md names `say-chain` as prone to stuck states; `check:liveness` found several ([checks.md](checks.md); the ledger `app/tools/liveness/ledger.json`).
- **`say-choice`**: the line before plays, then three balloons (the line and its two alternates, shuffled); the kid says one, it wins with a reaction (« LA VRAIE ! » or a funny one) and the next line plays. No wrong move; the third unrecognised try picks the closest by score (`choice.scores`). The compact microphone control sits in a sticky dock (cyan: the control's label is yellow while it waits) so it stays reachable under three tall balloons on a phone, its button and its tag, countdown and Pause on one row; whenever the dock grows (a note, the listening control) the balloons are scrolled back above it. While the microphone listens the balloons' play and pick buttons step aside (a play would go into the take), so on a phone the three balloons fit above the dock; with three long lines on a 390×844 phone the first row of the first balloon still scrolls away.

**"Je ne peux pas parler"** is on every speaking round at every step (`data-cant-speak`): before the permission, while recording, after a failed try, in listen-back and on the denied-microphone screen. It calls `practiceCtx.setSpeak(false)`: the setting is saved in the profile (`PATCH /api/me`), later practices are composed without microphone puzzles, and the shell swaps this and every later microphone puzzle of the practice for its `alt`. Speaking comes back with the profile switch or the microphone toggle in the header.

- **The replacement is composed, not improvised**: `replaceMicTask` (`shared/practice/replace.ts`) builds every microphone puzzle's `alt` from a fork of the rng, without touching the practice's used lines; alt ids are `<puzzle id>~<type>`. The shell reports the alt's own outcome (no skip, no mistake for the swap).
- **What replaces what**: `say-line`, `act-line` and `say-choice` → `rebuild` of that line; `say-hidden` → `fill-blanks` on its line with exactly that unit blanked (recalling the expression among distractors is the tapping form closest to producing it); `say-chain` → `match` on its units. Fallbacks in order: the other of `rebuild`/`fill-blanks` on the same line, `listen-pick` on the line, `match` with that expression or the line's key units, any tapping type on the scene, `watch`.
- The validator requires an `alt` on every microphone puzzle (same scene, no microphone) and validates it like any puzzle; both bots play it.

**Testing without speaking** (dev builds only): `?transcript=<French>` on the page URL is sent as `x-test-transcript` (a worker in `TEST_MODE` uses it instead of Whisper; the fixture client scores it with `scoreSpeech`); `?transcript=!unavailable` makes the fixture client answer 503. Chromium's `--use-fake-device-for-media-stream --use-fake-ui-for-media-stream` give a granted fake microphone, but its own sound is a 35 ms beep twice a second, which the voice gate rightly takes for clicks: the end-to-end tests add `--use-file-for-fake-audio-capture` with the speech-like loop of `e2e/fakeVoice.ts` (1.2 s of syllables, 1 s of quiet), generated when `playwright.config.ts` loads. In the liveness explorer the scripted recorder (`web/src/testing/fakes.ts`) decides the takes: a voice, silent, one that runs to the limit (`long`), too short, cut off. Against a worker, the scene must be unlocked for the kid. `/dev/speaking` is the user's lab for trying recognition with their own voice ([app-dev.md](app-dev.md)).

## Effects and sounds

`web/src/fx` (`index.ts` lists them): moments are comic impacts, not ticks. `fx.right(el)` on the right move (pop, a small impact with a sound word, the right sound); `fx.wrong(el)` on a wrong one (wobble, « OUPS ! », never a scolding); `fx.impact(word?, el?, { size })` for a big burst (PAF ! BOUM ! BRAVO !); particles, confetti, combos and floating text; `fx.sfx(name)` for synthesised sounds and the files in `/media/sfx`. ## Karaoke: singing along with the microphone

What the user asked for (2026-11-15): on the karaoke, record yourself singing along; if there is a voice it is sent to Whisper and checked for profanities only ("singing something different is fine"); if all is good you hear yourself over the song; the singing can be kept or removed, and a kept recording plays with the song every time the karaoke plays for you. Words (`AGENTS.md`): song, take (what the microphone just captured: a word for code and docs; the interface says « ton chant » / "your singing"), recording (the kept one; one per kid and song; erased, « effacer »). The server side and the check: [app-server.md](app-server.md), "Karaoke recordings".

**Where**: the full player only (`/karaoke/:id`, and the first song's player on `/karaoke`), in the studio under it (`web/src/karaoke/`: `useStudio.ts` holds the take's life, `StudioPanel.tsx` draws it, its `Studio` mounts it and tells the player what plays over the song and when its controls rest). Home's compact card mounts no studio and no recorder; it plays the recording. The song picker sits under the studio, and the stage's height follows the screen's (`Karaoke.module.css`), so the record control is on screen on arrival at 390×844, 768×1024, 1024×768, 1180×820 and 1440×900; on a 375×667 phone only its top edge shows above the tab bar (a short scroll).

**The states** (`data-studio` on the panel; `useStudio`'s `StudioPhase`), each with one obvious thing to do and a way out. Whenever the studio is not recording and not listening, the song plays as the karaoke screen always does, with the message readable under it.

| Phase | What shows | Ways on |
|---|---|---|
| `idle` | the microphone control (« Enregistre-toi ! »), a line on what it does (headphones advised); with a recording, its stamp and « Effacer » (a confirmation dialog first) | record; the song's own controls |
| `intro` | the first time a kid records a song, whatever they met before (its own flag, `frx.karaoke.intro`): the karaoke's notice, true to what happens (the singing is sent to be checked, kept only if they choose to keep it, then plays with the song, just for them), « D'accord ! » and « Retour à la chanson » (so the browser's Block, which blocks the microphone for the whole site in Chrome, is never the only way to say no) | OK: the microphone opens; back to the song |
| `recording` | the control listening: meter, « Je t'écoute ! », a countdown of the song's time left, Pause. The song runs once from its start with nothing over it; the player's controls, the bar, the rows and the picker rest. A refused or missing microphone shows its notice instead (« Retour à la chanson » works: the recorder is `lazy`, so a missing microphone says nothing before a tap and logs nothing; the deaf notice after three takes with no voice) | the button: done; Pause: dropped; the song's end: done; leaving the song (another song by the back button or a swipe, another screen): dropped, the microphone released |
| `silent`, `short` | the voice gate heard no voice (or too little): « On ne t'a pas entendu chanter ! »; nothing was sent | record again |
| `checking` | the control's « Je vérifie… » (the microphone is off) and « Je le jette »; the song plays on | waits at most `KARAOKE_TAKE_TIMEOUT_MS` (45 s), then `failed`; « Je le jette » abandons the request and drops the server's copy |
| `refused` | « Oups ! Il y a des mots interdits là-dedans. » (never the word); the singing is gone | record again |
| `failed` | the error panel: the kid's message (« La machine qui vérifie ne marche pas… » for a 503), the detail line, « Réessayer » (the same singing sent again without singing again) and « Je le jette »; for today's limit (429, « Reviens demain ») and singing too long to send (413) no « Réessayer », which could not work, only « Retour à la chanson » | retry, throw away |
| `listen`, `saving` | « C'EST TOI ! »: the song loops with the singing over it, « Je le garde ! », « Je refais », « Je le jette » | keep, do it again, throw away |

Singing never reaches `listen` without the server's verdict; the old recording stays until new singing is kept; throwing it away, and leaving `checking`, `failed` or `listen` without choosing (another song, another screen), deletes the server's copy, best effort (a failed delete is not shown: the copy waits for the next take or the daily purge, [app-server.md](app-server.md), "What is kept"). Keeping a take the server already kept (a lost answer) answers it again. The panel scrolls into view, clear of the tab bar, whenever it needs a tap. While the microphone listens, the line being sung comes up under the top bar and the control stays above the tab bar at every supported size (390×844, 375×667, 768×1024, 1024×768, 1180×820, 1440×900; the control is compact at every size for that: at 1024×768 a full control once pushed the words above the screen for the whole take; `e2e/karaoke.worker.spec.ts` measures it). Starting a take unmutes the app (the user's decision, pending: singing along needs the song). Updates of the app are held (`holdUpdates`) from the record tap (the notice included) to the singing kept or thrown away ([app-release.md](app-release.md)).

**The voice over the song**: whatever plays the song (start, loop, restart, a seek, a tapped row, Home's card) passes the recording as the engine's overlay (above, "Audio"): the song never waits for it (a slow download of up to 1.7 MB once held Home's card back 4 s), and the voice joins when it has loaded. `recordingOverlay()` gives it, and a page keeps its own take's audio for a recording it just kept, so nothing is downloaded (revoked when the recording is erased or replaced). When the overlay changes while the song plays (kept, erased, singing thrown away, a recording made on another device arriving with the state), the song goes on from where it is with the right voice. The stamp « AVEC TA VOIX » shows only while the voice is really in the mix (not while it loads, nor when it could not be loaded).

**Timing** (`shared/karaoke.ts`, "Timing"): the microphone opens first, then the song starts; the recorder notes when the take's first sample reached the microphone and the engine when the song's 0 was heard, both on `performance.now()`; the part of the take before the song's 0, and after its end, is cut, so the stored offset is in practice 0. On playback song and voice go through the same engine, so nothing is added there. In a digital loopback (the engine's output as the microphone, `e2e/karaoke.worker.spec.ts`) the take lands within 25 ms of where it was sung (the test allows 50). What a browser does not report (input latency outside Chrome; Bluetooth output latency on some devices) puts the voice that much late; unverified on a real device ([app-status.md](app-status.md)).

**Echo and the voice gate**: the microphone asks for echo cancellation (as in the speaking puzzles). Without headphones a silent kid may still leave the song's echo in the take: the gate (made for speech) may count it as a voice, Whisper then hears the lyrics or a stock phrase, and the take passes; the kid hears the song with a faint copy of itself. Harmless, so not guarded against; the idle line advises headphones.

**Tests**: `shared/karaoke.test.ts` (the check, the timing arithmetic), `tools/karaoke-bundle.test.ts` (the check over every piece of the show's French), `web/src/speech/wav.test.ts` (`sliceWav`), `worker/karaoke.test.ts`, `e2e/karaoke.worker.spec.ts` ([app-dev.md](app-dev.md)). Test ids: `[data-studio]` (`data-studio-recording`, `data-mic-status`), `[data-studio-record]` (the microphone button), `[data-studio-note]`, `[data-studio-keep]`, `[data-studio-redo]`, `[data-studio-drop]` (throw away, or back to the song after a 429 or 413), `[data-studio-remove]`, `[data-studio-confirm]`, `[data-studio-remove-go]`, `[data-studio-remove-keep]`, `[data-studio-mine]`, the notice's `[data-mic-intro]`, `[data-mic-ok]`, `[data-mic-skip]`; on the player `data-karaoke-overlay`, `data-karaoke-locked`, `[data-karaoke-voice]`, `[data-karaoke-stage]`, `[data-row="current"]`; toasts `karaoke-kept`, `karaoke-removed`.

## Notes on particular puzzle types

- **`listen-pick`** (« Qui dit quoi ? »): the scene's frame is the backdrop, with an empty balloon; the meanings are speech balloons; the right one flies into the balloon, and the prompt says « C'est ce que ça veut dire ! ». All five balloons always show at once, on every layout (the user's decision, intent.md, "Puzzles"); with long meanings on a phone the puzzle column scrolls (up to about 450 characters: two screens on 360×740). Tapping the right one first scrolls the picture back into view at once, then a copy flies from where the finger was to the empty balloon. `/dev/types_of_puzzles` has a second example with long choices (`EXTRA` in `DevPuzzleTypes.tsx`).
- **`question`** (« le quiz du druide »): the druid (`puzzles/question/QuizDruid.tsx`, faces from `pwa/druid.ts`) asks in a balloon; the answers are game-show cards whose French is `GlossText`. **Choose, then confirm** (the user's design): a tap on a card or on a box in it chooses that answer (the card lifts with a halo) and plays its clip once, then the question loops again; another tap moves the choice. « C'EST ÇA ! » under the board (`[data-confirm]`, disabled and asking to choose until a card is chosen; a choice scrolls it into view) submits it, and only then does the logic see a `pick`. The choice lives in the view, not in the state. The root's `data-step` is `choose`, `chosen` or `done`. A wrong answer deflates under a « RATÉ » stamp and clears the choice; the right one shows big with its English in the druid's balloon; BRAVO never covers the sentence.
- **`fill-blanks`**: a tapped word that fits another empty blank goes there (unless the kid chose a blank first); the logic's actions always name the blank.

**The arcade types**, invented for the app: shared helpers in `shared/puzzles/arcade.ts` (unit and line ranking, wrong choices by the rule in "Choices", French comparison), one test file `shared/puzzles/arcade.test.ts`, art and the fly-in animation in `web/src/puzzles/arcade/kit.tsx`. All keep the help strip closed and honour the used lines and units.

Each type scales with the practice's level through a small function at the top of its file (`rebuildRange`, `comicPanels`, `catapultShape`, `vraiFauxCards`, `memoryPairs`).

| Type | What the kid does | Grows with the level | `build` returns null when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rebuild` | A line loops; tap its shuffled pieces in the order heard. | more tiles from gold and diamond; single-word tiles first from titanium | no line has a usable number of distinct pieces |
| `comic-order` | 3–4 consecutive lines loop; tap their shuffled panels in order. | 4 panels from titanium | fewer than three usable consecutive lines, the stretch over 24 s, or two panels with the same French |
| `catapult` | An expression loops; knock down the Roman holding the right English sign. | more signs from gold, more rounds from diamond | fewer than two key units whose English differs from the French |
| `vrai-faux` | An expression loops with one English meaning; slam VRAI or FAUX. | more cards from gold and diamond | fewer than four usable key units |
| `memory` | Face-down shields, French and English; find the pairs. The backs say the language: French blue with dots and a « FR » speech balloon, English orange with stripes and an « EN » sign. While one shield is up, the others of its language are locked (dimmed, a tap only wobbles) and the prompt asks for the other language. A found pair's shields wear a tick badge on their corner and no speaker, so a long French text keeps the whole face on a phone. On a tablet the shields are big (square, up to about a seventh of the screen's height) in even rows: upright 2 to 5 columns by the number of cards, in landscape 3. | from gold the French shows no text until matched (by ear); more pairs from titanium and diamond | fewer than three usable key units apart in meaning |

**The opener and the cross-line types** (helpers `usableLine`, `linesConfusable` in `arcade.ts`; `linesConfusable` is looser than `nearlyEqual` on purpose, because song verses often start alike):

| Type | What the kid does | `build` returns null when |
|---|---|---|
| `place-words` | The whole scene loops with its dialogue, each line a panel in its bar colour. Up to five pieces (key units, one per line first, distinct texts) wait in a tray; the gaps are white dashed slots with a « + ». Either order: tap a piece (it speaks; again, its English), then the gap where it is heard; or tap a gap (it turns ink, its line loops), then its piece. At the end the scene plays once, whole. On a tablet in landscape the picture and the tray are a column beside the dialogue; on an upright tablet everything is one column as on a phone, the picture larger, and the tray after the dialogue sticks to the bottom so it is in reach from every line. | fewer than two key units with distinct text |
| `next-line` | A line loops; pick the line that follows among three; both then play back to back. | fewer than three lines, or no pair of following lines told apart |
| `line-meaning` | 3 or 4 lines as numbered speakers with their French hidden; tap a speaker (it loops), then its meaning. | no run of 3 usable consecutive lines told apart |

- **Wrong moves**: after two on one item the answer glows. `vrai-faux` glows the other button after one wrong answer. In `memory`, a shield of the same language as the one up is ignored (`lockedSide`; the bots never try it); a French and an English shield that do not match are a mistake only when the first shield's partner had been seen; a mismatched pair stays face up until the next tap (the logic never waits for an animation). Its root's `data-step` is `first`, `second`, `mismatch` or `done`.
- **Animations lag the logic**: `catapult` and `vrai-faux` advance in the reducer at once and keep the previous round on screen while it animates; code that reads the state must not assume what is on screen.
- **Distractors** in `catapult` and `vrai-faux` are other units' English (this scene, other scenes, then the bank), by the rule in "Choices": never close in meaning to the answer, never from related French, never close to the English of a unit of the scene with related French, never close to each other.
- **Targeting**: `next-line`, `line-meaning` and `comic-order` keep only stretches containing `targetLineId`; `catapult`, `vrai-faux` and `memory` take that line's units (smaller minimums when targeted); `rebuild` takes that line at any tile count that fits; `place-words` ignores targets.
- **Tap, then tap**: `place-words` and `line-meaning` are two-tap puzzles; a second tap on the selected piece keeps it selected and says it again. No dragging.
- **`place-words` in either order.** The action names both the piece and the gap; the state counts a wrong move on the piece (`wrong`, the mistakes) and on the gap (`wrongGap`), and `placeReveal` gives the answer of whatever is selected after two wrong moves on it: the picked piece's gap glows (scrolled to the middle of the screen: in a long scene it may be lines away or under the sticky tray), or the selected gap's piece glows in the tray (scrolled into view). Where the tray sticks to the bottom (a phone, an upright tablet) it covers the end of the dialogue: a selected gap is scrolled into sight above it, and when a piece is picked with every open gap out of sight (scrolled away or under the tray) the nearest one is (the gaps' `scroll-margin-bottom` is the tray's height, `--tray-h`). A picked piece is scrolled fully into the sideways tray, its « ? » tag too. The first-time tip by the tray is small there. After a wrong move the item chosen first stays selected; a wrong piece for a selected gap speaks once, then the gap's line loops again. While a piece is picked the open gaps beckon; while a gap is selected the tray pulses and a caption on its edge asks for the word (absolute, so nothing moves under the finger). A selected gap is let go by tapping it again, choosing another gap, or selecting another line. A tray piece speaks under the tag `piece:<unit id>` (`tags.piece`), and the bar and the slideshow hold while it plays (`holdWhile`): where it is heard is the answer, so neither the playhead, the line named nor the picture may jump there. A tap on a line's panel loops it (as in "A whole scene: line panels"); a tapped box in a line (or a placed piece) says itself once under its `unit:` tag, and may move the bar since it is on screen; then the looping line, or the scene from where it was, goes on; the play button pauses whatever plays, or plays the scene on from the last looped line. Attributes in every build: `data-step` on the root (`idle`, `piece`, `gap`, `done`), `data-gap-on` on the selected gap, `data-line-loop` on each panel's tap target, `data-looping` on the looping line, `data-bar-loop` on its bar segment.
- **Dev attributes** (dev builds only) for Playwright: `data-next` on the right tile or panel, `data-correct` on Romans, VRAI/FAUX and quiz cards, `data-pair` on shields (and, in every build, `data-side` on shields, `data-answer` on quiz cards, `data-confirm` on « C'EST ÇA ! »), `data-piece`/`data-gap`, `data-choice`, `data-speaker`/`data-meaning`. The shell's continue button also carries `data-next`, so select `[data-tile][data-next="true"]`, never `[data-next]` alone. `e2e/drive.ts` maps each type's `solve()` actions to these.

## Testing a puzzle type

- Unit tests of the logic, then `npm run check:practices` and `npm run check:liveness` ([checks.md](checks.md)).
- The `/dev` portal opens one puzzle type on one scene in the real shell without the server, chains several, aims one at a line or units, and runs a real practice through the API; `/dev/scene` shows the strip variants, the effects and the engine state ([app-dev.md](app-dev.md)).
- In dev builds `window.__frxAudio` is the engine: Playwright can read `getState()` (`src`, `tag`, `loop`, `startMs`, `endMs`, `playing`) and `getPositionMs()`. A view may add `data-*` attributes for tests only when `import.meta.env.DEV`.
- Elements that pulse (a looping box, the asterisk) never become "stable" for Playwright: click them with `{ force: true }`.
- Look at screenshots at 390×844, 1024×768 and 768×1024 against the art direction ([app-style.md](app-style.md)).

## Gotchas

- A box's inner text must be wrapped in one inline element: in an `inline-flex` button, whitespace between child spans is dropped (a highlight once rendered "ETSITUN'AIMESPAS").
- React runs a child's effects before its parent's: a parent that stops audio in its own effect after a puzzle change silences the child's freshly started loop. Stop audio in the cleanup of the component that goes away.
- Under `VITE_FIXTURES=1` the fixture API forgets practices on reload, so a practice reopened after a reload says it is finished and goes Home; the real server keeps them.
- `PracticeRunner`'s `onPuzzleDone` may return a promise (the practice screen's save); the shell waits for it before the next puzzle. The `/dev` pages return nothing, so they never wait.
